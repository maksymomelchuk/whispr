# Meeting mode ("AI note taker") — research + plan

Status: research done 2026-11-20; the MVP (phase 1) is built. Decisions made after this write-up:

- Transcription: Soniox async (`stt-async-v6`) after the call only; no live transcript yet.
- Notes: written by any AI Provider already connected on the Cleanup page, picked the same way Profiles pick cleanup (default Anthropic + Claude Sonnet 5.5). Anthropic goes through the existing transport, including the OAuth credential — the owner's call for this private, single-user app, knowing Anthropic's terms restrict subscription tokens in third-party apps.
- Push-to-talk keeps working during a meeting (separate mic recorder); output is no longer muted while a meeting records.
  Legend: **[doc]** = stated in vendor docs/coverage, **[inferred]** = my reading, not documented.

## 1. How others do it

### Wispr Flow — Notetaker (launched Aug 2026, beta)

- **Bot-free.** Runs on your machine. Mic = you, system audio = everyone else. No bot joins. [doc] — [Getting started](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/3665250541-getting-started-with-notetaker-beta), [What is system audio capture?](https://wisprflow.ai/notetaker/what-is-system-audio-capture)
- **Permissions:** Microphone + System Audio Recording on Mac. Flow has to be restarted after granting system audio. Their docs say Ventura+; a third-party review says 14.4+. [doc, sources disagree] The restart requirement and the dedicated "System Audio Recording" pane suggest **Core Audio process taps** (macOS 14.2+, prompt on 14.4+), not ScreenCaptureKit. [inferred]
- **Live + post-call passes.** Live transcript while the call runs. After the call, "processes the full recording again with additional context" to get a better transcript and better summary/decisions/action items. Audio is uploaded (the upload step can stall on a bad network). [doc] — [Live transcript](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4091106439-the-live-transcript-in-notetaker-beta), [Flow Summaries](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/1422535682-flow-summaries-in-notetaker-beta)
- **Speakers:** the live labels come from the audio source: "You" = mic, "Them" = system audio. Several voices on the mic become You (Speaker 1/2). Real names are added after the call from calendar attendees, and Wispr says those names are guesses ("not proof of who spoke"). [doc]
- **Context:** reuses the personal dictionary (acronyms, product names) plus calendar and Slack context. [doc] — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/05/wispr-flow-takes-on-ai-meeting-assistants-with-notetaker-its-first-product-beyond-dictation/), [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4206765/wispr-moves-beyond-ai-dictation-with-note-taking-assistant.html)
- **Output:** a Summary tab kept separate from typed notes. It covers decisions, key dates/deadlines, action items/owners, and open issues. Also "ask across meetings" and an MCP export. [doc] — [Privacy overview](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4497184932-notetaker-privacy-and-security-overview)
- Consent is left to the user, because nobody on the call is notified. [doc]

### macOS capture options (the hard part)

1. **Core Audio process taps** (`AudioHardwareCreateProcessTap` + an aggregate device), **macOS 14.2+**. Audio only. Can tap one process (by PID) or the whole system mix. Uses its own "System Audio Recording Only" permission (`NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription`), with a prompt on 14.4+. Lower overhead than ScreenCaptureKit, and it catches audio that SCK misses. Downsides: thin Apple docs, and there are reports of all-zero buffers in some setups. — [Apple forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/756428), [AudioCap sample](https://github.com/insidegui/AudioCap), [Recall.ai write-up](https://www.recall.ai/blog/core-audio-taps), [zero-buffers thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825780)
   - Rust: the `cidre` crate exposes `ca::TapDesc`. The open-source Tauri meeting app **Meetily** uses this path (`src-tauri/src/audio/capture/core_audio.rs`), so there's a Tauri precedent. — [car-voice source ported from Meetily](https://docs.rs/crate/car-voice/0.6.0/source/src/system_audio_capture.rs)
2. **ScreenCaptureKit** audio (macOS 13+). Needs the full "Screen & System Audio Recording" permission, and the scary screen-recording prompt comes with it. Rust: the `screencapturekit` crate (v8; has had many breaking releases). — [docs.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/screencapturekit/8.0.1)
3. **Virtual audio device** (BlackHole/Loopback, as Krisp does). Needs a driver install and makes the user re-route audio. Too much friction, so rejected.
4. **Bot that joins the call** (Otter, Recall.ai). Needs server infra and is visible to everyone on the call. Out of scope for a personal desktop app.

### Long audio and summary patterns

- **Live + final pass** (Wispr): stream for the live view, then re-transcribe the saved file after the call for quality. This gives the best accuracy and stays robust if the live socket drops.
- **Source-separated tracks** give "You vs Them" with no diarization model. Diarization is only needed _within_ the system track, when there are several remote people.
- Soniox: realtime $0.12/h, async $0.10/h, diarization included (up to 15 speakers), and **realtime WS sessions are capped at 60 min** (so you have to reconnect). — [Soniox pricing](https://soniox.com/compare/stt/pricing), [diarization](https://soniox.com/speech-to-text/features/diarization)
- A 90-min meeting comes to roughly 12–15k words, about 20k tokens [inferred]. That fits in one LLM call, so no map-reduce is needed below ~3 h.
- Summaries are shaped by templates (Granola) and a fixed set of sections (Wispr: summary / decisions / dates / action items / open questions).

## 3. Recommended approach

**Capture**: two separate tracks, **mic** (existing cpal recorder, its own instance) and **system** (a new Core Audio process tap of the system mix via `cidre`, macOS 14.2+). Don't mix them. Keeping them separate gives "You / Them" labels for free and lets each go to STT on its own. Stream both to disk in rolling FLAC segments (e.g. 5 min each) so a crash loses at most one segment. Never mute output. Windows/Linux: system loopback is possible later (WASAPI loopback), but not in the MVP.

**Transcription** (cloud default, Soniox):

- MVP: **post-call only**. On stop, send each track to the **Soniox async API** with diarization on the system track. Merge the two tracks' tokens by timestamp into `[mm:ss] You: … / Speaker 2: …`. About $0.10/h per track, so roughly $0.20 for a 1-hour meeting. Soniox handles UK↔EN code-switching, which is the user's real need (see stt-code-switching memory).
- Phase 2: a **live transcript** over Soniox realtime WS per track, reconnecting every ~55 min (cap is 60). Keep the post-call async pass as the source of truth, the same pattern Wispr uses.
- Local option (privacy/offline): Parakeet v3 on 30-s chunks with VAD. Free, but code-switching is weaker and there's no diarization (You/Them still works because the tracks are separate). Offer it later as a setting.

**Summary**: reuse the AI Provider plumbing (same keys, Anthropic native or OpenAI-compatible), with a new `meeting_summary` call: its own prompt, `max_tokens ≈ 4096`, a ~120 s timeout, and a model picked in settings (default: the configured Anthropic model; a Sonnet-class model works better than Haiku for 20k-token inputs [inferred]). Single pass up to ~3 h. Beyond that, map-reduce per 30-min block.
Prompt shape: system = role + rules ("only facts said in the transcript; attribute to speaker label; mark uncertainty; reply in the meeting's dominant language unless set"), plus a glossary from Term sets. User message = the merged transcript. Output is Markdown with fixed sections: **TL;DR (3 bullets) · Key points · Decisions · Action items (owner — task — due) · Open questions**. Later: user-editable templates (Granola-style), a "regenerate with template X" button, and a "chat with this meeting" view.

**Storage/UI**: `app_data/meetings/{id}/` holds `mic-NNN.flac`, `system-NNN.flac`, `transcript.json` (segments: start, end, speaker, text), `notes.md`, and `meta.json` (title, started_at, duration, cost, status). A separate retention setting from dictation history. Add a Meetings page with a list, plus a detail view with Notes / Transcript / Audio tabs and Copy-as-Markdown. Start and stop from the tray, a dedicated hotkey (`HotkeyAction::Meeting`), and the Meetings page. Show a recording dot in the tray while a meeting is recording.

## 4. Code changes & phases

**Phase 1 — MVP (record → post-call transcript → summary), macOS only** (~1.5–2k LOC incl. tests, rough)

- `src-tauri/src/system_audio.rs` (new, macOS): `cidre` process tap to i16 chunks, same `AudioFormat` contract as `recorder.rs`. ~300 LOC.
- `recorder.rs`: allow a second concurrent stream (or a separate `Recorder::spawn()` instance for meetings). Small change.
- `meeting/` (new module): `capture.rs` (two tracks to rolling FLAC segments, reusing `groq_audio::encode_to_flac_16k_mono`), `store.rs` (meetings dir + meta), `transcribe.rs` (Soniox async client: upload, poll, tokens with speaker + ms), `merge.rs` (pure: interleave two token streams into speaker turns; easy to unit-test), `summary.rs` (prompt + call). ~900 LOC.
- `cleanup.rs` / `cleanup_invoke.rs`: make max_tokens/timeout/system prompt parameters instead of constants, so summary reuses the transport. ~100 LOC diff.
- `config.rs`: `MeetingSettings { stt_provider, summary_provider, summary_model, retention }` + `HotkeyAction::Meeting`. `permissions.rs` + `Info.plist`: audio-capture permission. `tray.rs`: Start/Stop meeting.
- Frontend: `src/pages/MeetingsPage.tsx` (+ detail), a nav entry, and a permission onboarding card. ~500 LOC.

**Phase 2 — live**: Soniox realtime per track, a live transcript pane, 55-min WS reconnect, a cost/time readout.
**Phase 3 — polish**: templates/recipes, calendar-based titles and attendee names, auto-detect calls (Zoom/Meet/Teams running plus mic in use), local STT option, Windows WASAPI loopback, "ask across meetings".

## 5. How it gets proven

- Unit: `merge.rs` (overlap, interleave, empty track), the summary prompt builder, the Soniox async response parser (mockito fixtures, as the existing session tests do), segment rotation, and crash recovery (orphan segments get picked up on next launch).
- Integration on a real Mac: a 2-person Google Meet/Zoom of ≥30 min (Maksym + a second device), headphones on and off. Check that both sides appear, "You/Them" is correct, the UK↔EN mix is preserved, and the action items match what was said.
- Endurance: a 90-min session (a YouTube talk playing as "system", mic speaking intermittently). Watch RSS memory stays flat (audio goes to disk, not RAM). Kill the app at ~40 min and confirm the segments survive and can be transcribed.
- Permission flows: a fresh user, a denied prompt then a re-grant, and the macOS <14.2 fallback message.

## 6. Risks / open questions

1. **Cloud vs local STT.** Recommend Soniox cloud for quality and code-switching (~$0.20/h for 2 tracks). Is uploading meeting audio OK for your work calls, or is local-only a hard requirement?
2. **Live transcript in the MVP or not?** Post-call only is half the work and is more robust. Live is a nicer experience.
3. **macOS floor:** process taps need 14.2+. Is that OK, or do you need ScreenCaptureKit (13+) as a fallback?
4. **Echo without headphones:** the mic picks up the other side, so their words get duplicated and labeled "You". Mitigations: Apple voice-processing AEC on the mic, or dedupe near-identical overlapping segments. For the MVP, recommend headphones.
5. **Consent:** bot-free recording doesn't notify anyone. Show a reminder and possibly a "post a recording notice" snippet.
6. **Summary model and language:** default output language (meeting language vs. always English)? Which model (Sonnet-class vs Haiku)?
7. **Dictation during a meeting:** must push-to-talk keep working while a meeting records? (This needs the two-recorder change. I'd recommend yes.)
8. Wispr-style real names need calendar access. Proposal: skip, with manual "rename Speaker 2 → Olena" instead.
